# Quality rubric

Use this rubric after drafting and before delivery. Revise any critical failure. Score each dimension from 0 to 3.

## Contents

- Scoring scale
- Ten quality dimensions
- Delivery gate
- Retention audit
- Read-aloud audit
- AI-pattern audit

## Scoring scale

- **0 — failed:** absent, misleading, or seriously harmful to the script
- **1 — weak:** present but generic, confusing, unsupported, or inconsistent
- **2 — solid:** clear and functional with minor room to improve
- **3 — strong:** specific, coherent, earned, and well matched to the format

## 1. Promise alignment

- Does the opening confirm the title and thumbnail premise?
- Does the body deliver the same value or story?
- Does the ending fulfill the promise at the right level of certainty?

Critical failure: bait-and-switch or a payoff weaker than the explicit claim.

## 2. Hook clarity

- Can a new viewer identify the subject or situation immediately?
- Is the tension, value, or anomaly specific?
- Does the hook create interest without requiring missing context?

Critical failure: generic hype, prolonged ambiguity, or setup before the subject.

## 3. Narrative causality

- Do beats connect through cause, conflict, evidence, consequence, or answer?
- Can the viewer understand why the next beat follows?
- Is chronology selected rather than dumped?

Critical failure: a list of events or facts with no governing question or causal spine.

## 4. Information progression

- Does each section add genuinely new value?
- Are context and explanation delivered when needed?
- Are mini-payoffs distributed instead of deferred indefinitely?

Critical failure: repetition, padding, or multiple beats that leave the story state unchanged.

## 5. Stakes and emotional movement

- Is it clear why the goal, answer, choice, or outcome matters?
- Do stakes change as the script develops?
- Does intensity vary rather than remain permanently elevated?

Critical failure: forced drama unsupported by consequence.

## 6. Payoff quality

- Are major setups resolved?
- Is the central payoff concrete and proportionate to the promise?
- Does the ending show consequence or meaning without overexplaining?

Critical failure: cliffhanger in place of requested resolution, or a generic moral instead of an answer.

## 7. Spoken naturalness

- Can each sentence be spoken cleanly once?
- Are names, numbers, clauses, and pronouns easy to follow by ear?
- Does sentence length vary naturally?
- Do line breaks support performance rather than simulate intensity?

Critical failure: prose that reads well silently but cannot be understood at the intended pace.

## 8. Specificity and originality

- Are key claims, images, examples, and consequences concrete?
- Has generic creator language been removed?
- Does the script have a distinct narrator stance rather than default “AI documentary” prose?

Critical failure: copied signature language or unsupported cinematic detail.

## 9. Factual integrity

- Is every material factual claim supported, qualified, or clearly framed as interpretation?
- Are quotations exact and attributed?
- Are chronology, causality, and uncertainty represented honestly?
- Are real people treated proportionately and fairly?

Critical failure: fabricated fact, quote, scene, motive, allegation, or source.

## 10. Format and timing

- Does the architecture fit long-form or short-form rather than merely the word count?
- Is the estimated length within a reasonable range?
- Has the actual or synthetic read been timed where possible?
- Does the script leave room for pauses and comprehension?

Critical failure: misses the requested duration materially or relies on an implausible speaking speed.

## Delivery gate

Before delivery, require:

- no score of 0;
- scores of at least 2 for promise alignment, hook clarity, payoff quality, factual integrity, and format/timing;
- total score of at least 24/30 for a complete draft;
- all critical failures resolved.

Do not show the numerical score unless the user asks for an evaluation. Use it to revise.

## Retention audit

Inspect the script by viewer state, not by decorative tricks.

At each beat ask:

1. What does the viewer now understand that they did not before?
2. What expectation was confirmed, changed, or overturned?
3. Which live question was answered?
4. Which next question follows naturally?
5. Is there a concrete reason to keep watching beyond “more is coming”?
6. Could this beat be moved earlier, compressed, combined, or deleted?

For a weak section, diagnose the likely cause:

- promise mismatch;
- excessive context;
- repeated information;
- unclear causality;
- predictable development;
- missing proof;
- unresolved pronouns or jargon;
- intensity without stakes;
- payoff delivered too late;
- payoff delivered before enough development;
- transition that resets rather than advances the story.

## Read-aloud audit

Read or synthesize the script at the intended pace. Mark:

- breath failures;
- tongue-twisting clusters;
- sentences that require rereading;
- ambiguous pronouns;
- numbers that sound alike;
- unearned dramatic pauses;
- abrupt tonal shifts;
- jokes whose setup is not audible;
- emotional claims unsupported by the preceding detail.

Rewrite the line, not merely the punctuation.

## AI-pattern audit

Search for:

- repeated “not X, but Y” constructions;
- a rhetorical question at every transition;
- three-adjective clusters;
- abstract moral language;
- identical paragraph lengths;
- excessive fragments;
- fake suspense phrases;
- constant escalation words such as “even more,” “incredible,” “massive,” and “shocking”;
- recaps that repeat the preceding section;
- omniscient claims about what people thought or felt;
- conclusions that announce significance instead of demonstrating it.

Keep any pattern that is earned and channel-specific. Rewrite automatic repetition.
