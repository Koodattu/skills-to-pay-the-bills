# Story engines

Choose one primary engine. Combine engines only when the combination is necessary and the transition is clear.

## Contents

- Investigation and reveal
- Causal explainer
- Documentary pressure narrative
- Challenge and transformation

- Profile through contradiction

- Escalating comparison

- Choosing quickly

## 1. Investigation and reveal

**Best for:** mysteries, failures, disputed explanations, hidden systems, scientific or historical questions.

**Spine:** anomaly → precise question → initial evidence → plausible explanation → conflicting evidence → narrowed possibilities → reveal → consequence.

**Viewer motion:** “What happened?” becomes “Which explanation survives?”

**Failure mode:** withholding known basics, presenting weak clues as equal, or inventing certainty.

## 2. Causal explainer

**Best for:** technology, science, economics, design, policy, systems, “why” videos.

**Spine:** surprising observation → why it matters → visible effect → hidden mechanism → example or test → limitation or exception → synthesis → implication.

**Viewer motion:** “That is strange” becomes “Now I can predict it.”

**Failure mode:** a textbook sequence with no concrete problem, proof, or complication.

## 3. Documentary pressure narrative

**Best for:** history, true events, institutions, disasters, projects, competitions.

**Spine:** defining moment → initial conditions → accumulating pressure → constrained choices → decisive action → immediate consequence → longer consequence → meaning.

**Viewer motion:** “How did they reach this moment?” becomes “Why did this outcome follow?”

**Failure mode:** chronology without selection, hindsight certainty, or invented scene detail.

## 4. Challenge and transformation

**Best for:** experiments, builds, personal journeys, gaming challenges, investigations with an active host.

**Spine:** goal → rules and failure condition → first plan → setback → learned constraint → revised plan → final test → result → changed understanding.

**Viewer motion:** “Can this be done?” becomes “What did the attempts reveal?”

**Failure mode:** repetitive attempts that do not change strategy, stakes, or character.

## 5. Argument under pressure

**Best for:** commentary, criticism, video essays, strategy, cultural analysis.

**Spine:** tension or claim → concrete case → governing reasoning → supporting evidence → strongest counterargument → refinement or concession → larger implication.

**Viewer motion:** “Is this claim true?” becomes “What version of the claim survives scrutiny?”

**Failure mode:** announcing a thesis and spending the rest of the video restating it.

## 6. Profile through contradiction

**Best for:** biographies, creator or company profiles, character studies.

**Spine:** defining contradiction → formative pressure → pursuit → recurring obstacle → decisive choice → cost or consequence → legacy or unresolved tension.

**Viewer motion:** “How can both sides of this person be true?” becomes “Which choice explains the arc?”

**Failure mode:** cradle-to-present chronology or unsupported psychological motive.

## 7. Case study and postmortem

**Best for:** business, products, engineering, campaigns, projects, failures, turnarounds.

**Spine:** notable outcome → starting position → hidden constraint → key decision → implementation → unexpected failure or success → adaptation → measured result → transferable lesson.

**Viewer motion:** “What produced the outcome?” becomes “Which decision mattered, under what conditions?”

**Failure mode:** outcome bias, hero worship, or a lesson broader than the evidence supports.

## 8. Escalating comparison

**Best for:** rankings, tool comparisons, product histories, levels, myths, multiple cases.

**Spine:** selection rule → baseline case → stronger or stranger case → changed criterion → decisive comparison → winner or synthesis → caveat.

**Viewer motion:** “How do these differ?” becomes “Which criterion actually decides?”

**Failure mode:** arbitrary ordering and interchangeable mini-reviews.

## 9. Reversal microstory

**Best for:** short-form stories, surprising facts, compact historical moments, mistakes.

**Spine:** expected outcome → concrete setup → apparent confirmation → one fact changes the interpretation → actual outcome → button.

**Viewer motion:** “I know where this is going” becomes “I was using the wrong model.”

**Failure mode:** a twist with no setup, evidence, or consequence.

## 10. Practical transformation

**Best for:** tutorials, advice, before-and-after, productivity, design, education.

**Spine:** costly problem → common failed approach → governing principle → concrete method → demonstration → boundary condition → result → next action.

**Viewer motion:** “Why does my approach fail?” becomes “I know what to do and when it works.”

**Failure mode:** generic tips, no demonstration, or a promise of universal results.

## Choosing quickly

Use this default mapping:

- “What happened?” → investigation or documentary pressure
- “Why does this happen?” → causal explainer
- “Can it be done?” → challenge and transformation
- “Is this claim right?” → argument under pressure
- “Why is this person or company notable?” → profile through contradiction
- “What can we learn from this result?” → case study and postmortem
- “Which is best or strangest?” → escalating comparison
- “How do I get from problem to result?” → practical transformation

When two engines seem plausible, choose the one whose central question most closely matches the clicked promise.
